Detailed Analysis Report: Namespace Decommissioning Incident

1. Incident Summary

A production namespace was incorrectly decommissioned, leading to significant disruptions and ~13 production issues.

The decommissioning process bypassed critical validation checks, resulting in deletion of active resources.

The issue stemmed from an incorrect namespace included in a work order and insufficient controls to verify ownership and activity.



---

2. Root Cause Analysis

The failure originated from multiple breakdowns across teams:

1. Incorrect Request Initiation

A non-platform requester mistakenly included the wrong namespace (belonging to another AID) in a work order.

The requester was not authorized or properly trained for namespace decommissioning.



2. Control Failure: AID–Namespace Alignment

The governance team failed to validate that the namespace mapped to the correct AID before approving deletion.

This was the first control gap.



3. Control Failure: Environment Confirmation (Production Check)

No secondary review was conducted for the production namespace before deletion.

This was the second control gap.



4. Control Failure: Active Resource Verification

Namespace was not checked for active workloads/resources before decommissioning.

The requester lacked access to confirm deletion, but the governance and platform teams did not verify independently.

This was the third control gap.



5. Platform (Serious/CDAS) Execution

Despite resources being active, the platform team proceeded with deletion once the work order was approved.





---

3. Control Failures (Summary)

AID to Namespace Mapping Validation → Missed.

Owner/Maintainer Verification → Not performed.

Production Namespace Escalation → No secondary managerial approval.

Empty Namespace Check → Ignored despite active resources.

Traffic/Activity Review → Not performed before decommissioning.



---

4. Impact Assessment

Business Impact: 13 production issues triggered, disrupting critical services.

Technical Impact:

Loss of namespace resources.

Possible Splunk log stream disconnections.

BuildConfigs, pod-level annotations, and configurations lost.

No backup mechanism identified for recovery.


Reputational Impact: Negative escalation, loss of confidence in governance process.



---

5. Corrective Actions Taken

Immediate engagement between Governance, Serious (platform), and Application teams to analyze failures.

Splunk integration verification initiated to ensure observability.

Restoration attempts for resources confirmed as non-recoverable.

Agreement to introduce stricter secondary approvals for production namespace decommissioning.



---

6. Recommendations & Next Steps

1. Strengthen Namespace Governance Controls

Implement mandatory AID–Namespace–Maintainer–Environment mapping validation before any deletion.

Introduce system-driven checks (automated alignment validation).



2. Owner Confirmation Process

Require explicit confirmation from the namespace owner/maintainer (not just the requester) before approval.

Maintain an updated owner registry with escalation paths.



3. Production Namespace Safeguards

Enforce secondary managerial approval for all production namespace decommissioning.

Automate traffic/activity verification (e.g., via TrueSight or weekly activity reports).



4. Platform/Serious Team Checks

Modify deletion workflows to block removal of namespaces with active resources.

Enforce platform-side validation independent of requester input.



5. Audit & Training

Audit recent decommissioning requests for similar risks.

Train requestors and governance staff on namespace lifecycle and approval hierarchy.



6. Recovery/Resilience

Explore snapshots/backups for critical configurations (pods, BuildConfigs, logs).

Ensure Splunk/logging integrations are automatically restored post-recreation.





---

7. Conclusion

This incident highlights systemic governance gaps in namespace lifecycle management. While processes existed, they were not enforced consistently, especially around production environments. By reinforcing ownership checks, automating validations, and enforcing secondary approvals, recurrence of such high-impact issues can be prevented.
